Four practical case studies

Different businesses. Useful next steps.

These illustrative scenarios show how focused strategy and hands-on digital work can be shaped around the real friction in a business.

Illustrative playbooks, not client testimonials or claims of completed work or measured results. The right scope and success measures depend on each business.

ONLINE RETAIL

Good products, but a leaky path to purchase.

The challenge

Product discovery, campaign promises, and the mobile store experience feel disconnected. Shoppers have to work too hard to understand the offer and choose confidently.

A practical approach
  • Align campaign messages with collection and product pages.
  • Clarify product details, trust cues, and the mobile buying path.

Useful signals: product-page engagement, add-to-cart behavior, customer questions, and checkout friction.

COACH · CONSULTANT · CREATOR

Strong expertise, unclear route to becoming a client.

The challenge

Content earns attention, but the offer, audience fit, and next step are spread across social posts, messages, and a hard-to-navigate website.

A practical approach
  • Shape one clear offer page around the audience's needs and questions.
  • Connect useful content to a simple enquiry or booking flow.

Useful signals: qualified enquiries, booking completion, common objections, and follow-up effort.

SERVICE · STUDIO · CLINIC

Nearby customers search, but don't know who to trust.

The challenge

Business details vary across listings, service information is vague, and contacting or booking requires too many steps.

A practical approach
  • Make local profiles, service pages, hours, and contact details consistent.
  • Show what to expect and make calling, messaging, or booking easy on mobile.

Useful signals: calls and direction requests, booking enquiries, profile accuracy, and missed-contact patterns.

GROWING BUSINESS

More channels create more work, not more clarity.

The challenge

Website, marketplace, marketing, and customer follow-up are managed in separate routines. Small gaps turn into duplicated effort and inconsistent customer experiences.

A practical approach
  • Map the handoffs and identify where information or ownership breaks down.
  • Agree a manageable operating rhythm, clear responsibilities, and a few useful checks.

Useful signals: recurring errors, delayed follow-up, stock or content mismatches, and time spent on repeat tasks.
