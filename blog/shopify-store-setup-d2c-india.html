Shopify Store Setup for D2C Brands in India: A Practical Guide

Suryaprakash Jangid8 min read

Most Shopify stores I get asked to fix weren't broken by bad marketing. They were broken on day one, during setup — a catalog structure that made sense to the founder and nobody else, a checkout with three unnecessary steps, or shipping rules that quietly excluded half the country. Ads and SEO can't fix a store that isn't set up to convert. Here's what actually matters, in the order it matters.

Pick the platform before you pick a theme

If you haven't committed to Shopify yet, it's worth reading our comparison of Shopify vs WooCommerce for Indian small businesses first — the platform decision affects almost everything below. This guide assumes you've landed on Shopify, which is usually the right call for a founder who wants to spend time on product and marketing, not server maintenance.

Get the catalog structure right before anything else

This is the step most stores rush, and it's the one that's hardest to fix later without breaking URLs and losing whatever SEO you'd built. Before adding a single product:

  • Decide your collection logic once. By category, by use-case, by collection/drop — pick one primary logic and stick to it, or customers get lost.
  • Standardise variants early. Size and colour options should use the same option names across every product, or your filters break.
  • Treat product photography as a conversion asset, not a formality. For jewelry, textile, and handicraft catalogs especially, a consistent background, consistent angle count (front, detail, in-use, scale reference), and consistent image dimensions make the whole store feel considered rather than thrown together.
  • Write descriptions for the decision, not the product. Materials and dimensions matter, but so does answering the question the customer is actually holding — "will this fit," "is this real silver," "how long will this last."

Payment gateways that actually matter in India

Shopify Payments isn't available in India, so this decision needs a deliberate choice, not a default. Razorpay and Cashfree are the two most commonly used gateways for Indian D2C stores, and both support UPI, cards, netbanking, and wallets in one checkout. If a meaningful share of your customers are in tier-2/3 cities, keep Cash on Delivery available — it still converts a large share of first-time buyers who haven't built trust in your brand yet, even if it adds RTO risk you'll need to manage separately.

Shipping and tax rules that trip people up

Two mistakes show up constantly: flat shipping rates that quietly lose money on heavy or remote-zone orders, and GST-inclusive pricing that isn't actually reflected correctly on the invoice. Set up zone-based shipping rates early (metro vs rest-of-India vs remote/northeast), and confirm your invoice template shows GST correctly before your first real order — not after your first support ticket about it.

The checkout mistakes that quietly kill conversion

A visitor who reaches checkout has already decided to buy. Losing them there is the most expensive kind of drop-off, because you already paid for the click. The usual culprits:

  • Forcing account creation before checkout instead of offering guest checkout
  • Shipping costs that only appear at the final step, not on the product or cart page
  • Too few payment options for how your specific customers actually pay
  • Slow-loading checkout on mobile, where most Indian D2C traffic actually is

If cart abandonment is already a problem for your store, the recovery flows that actually bring buyers back are worth setting up alongside these fixes — recovery and prevention work best together, not as a substitute for each other.

SEO foundations to set on day one

You don't need a full SEO campaign before launch, but a few things are far easier to set up correctly from the start than to retrofit: unique title tags and meta descriptions per product and collection page (not the Shopify default), descriptive alt text on every product image, and a clean URL structure that doesn't change later. One caveat worth knowing early: Google Business Profile listings require in-person customer contact to qualify — a store that's entirely online-only, with no storefront or client visits, generally isn't eligible for a Maps listing, so plan your discoverability strategy around organic search and marketplaces instead of assuming a Maps pin is coming.

A store that's easy to browse on a slow phone connection will usually outperform a beautifully designed one that isn't.
