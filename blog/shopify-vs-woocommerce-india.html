Shopify vs WooCommerce for Indian Small Businesses: Which Should You Pick

Suryaprakash Jangid7 min read

This is usually the first real decision a new D2C founder has to make, and it gets more conflicting advice online than almost anything else in the setup process. There isn't a universally "better" platform — there's a better platform for your specific situation. Here's how to actually decide.

Cost: Shopify is predictable, WooCommerce is variable

Shopify's monthly subscription bundles hosting, security, and updates into one predictable cost. WooCommerce itself is free, but it's a plugin on top of WordPress — so you're paying for hosting, an SSL certificate, and usually a handful of paid plugins (for SEO, backups, and payment gateway integration) separately. For a founder who wants one bill and no server decisions, Shopify is simpler. For a founder comfortable piecing together a stack to keep monthly costs lower, WooCommerce can end up cheaper at small scale.

Ease of setup and maintenance

Shopify is built to be managed without technical help — themes, apps, and settings are all in one dashboard, and updates happen automatically. WooCommerce requires more ongoing attention: plugin updates, occasional conflicts between plugins, and WordPress security maintenance are your responsibility unless you're paying a developer or agency to handle it. If you don't have technical support on call, that maintenance burden is worth weighing seriously against the lower sticker price.

Payment gateways and COD support in India

Both platforms support Razorpay and Cashfree, and both can support Cash on Delivery — this isn't a major differentiator anymore the way it was a few years ago. Where it does matter: Shopify's checkout is more locked down and optimised out of the box, while WooCommerce checkout customisation is more flexible but requires more setup work to reach the same level of polish.

Scalability and catalog size

Shopify handles traffic spikes and large catalogs without the store owner needing to think about server capacity — that's handled on Shopify's end. WooCommerce scales too, but performance depends heavily on your hosting quality; a large catalog on cheap shared hosting will feel noticeably slower than the same catalog on Shopify, and fixing that later usually means a hosting migration, not a quick setting change.

So which should you pick?

As a practical rule of thumb:

  • Pick Shopify if you want to focus on product and marketing without managing technical infrastructure, you're planning to scale catalog size or traffic significantly, or you don't have a developer on call.
  • Pick WooCommerce if you already have a WordPress site with content or SEO equity you don't want to lose, you're comfortable with (or have access to) some technical maintenance, or your catalog and traffic are modest enough that hosting performance won't become a bottleneck soon.

Once you've picked, the setup details matter more than the platform choice itself — see our Shopify store setup guide for what to get right from day one, most of which applies conceptually to WooCommerce too.

The platform rarely makes or breaks a store. Catalog structure, checkout friction, and follow-up do.
